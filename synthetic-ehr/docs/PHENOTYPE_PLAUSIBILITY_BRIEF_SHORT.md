# Synthetic EHR Phenotype Plausibility Brief (Concise)
Date: February 11, 2026

The synthetic cohort is designed for clinical plausibility and internal consistency, not prevalence matching. Across all phenotypes, the generator enforces key relationships: HbA1c is aligned with glucose using ADAG logic, CKD stage is derived from eGFR, albuminuria stage is derived from UACR, and kidney chemistries are constrained to move coherently with renal function. Diabetes type rules constrain expected C-peptide and insulin patterns (for example, low C-peptide with mandatory insulin in type 1 diabetes). The result is a teaching-ready synthetic dataset that behaves like realistic clinical profiles while remaining transparent and tunable [1-9].

**Metabolic syndrome, no diabetes, no CKD (`metabolic_syndrome_no_dm_ckd`)**: This phenotype represents obesity-associated insulin resistance with cardiometabolic risk factor clustering but without overt diabetes or clinically significant kidney disease. Typical patterns include elevated BMI, borderline glycemia (A1c/fasting glucose near prediabetes thresholds), high triglycerides, lower HDL, and mildly elevated blood pressure. UACR generally remains in A1 and eGFR is preserved, consistent with absence of diabetic kidney disease. This profile is useful for teaching that substantial metabolic risk can exist before formal diabetes diagnosis and why early risk-factor modification is clinically important [1,2,4,6-8].

**Early type 2 diabetes, minimal kidney involvement (`early_t2d_no_ckd`)**: This phenotype models newly established T2D with modest hyperglycemia, preserved endogenous insulin secretion, and limited renal injury. HbA1c and fasting/random glucose are in diabetic ranges, but eGFR is usually normal-to-mildly reduced and UACR commonly remains A1. Lipid and blood pressure abnormalities are present in a metabolic-syndrome pattern, and metformin-centered therapy is plausible at this stage. It is a good instructional profile for discussing early T2D pathophysiology, treatment entry points, and prevention of downstream renal and cardiovascular complications [1,2,4,6-9].

**Type 2 diabetes with obesity and early diabetic kidney disease (`t2d_obesity_ckd2`)**: This phenotype represents progression from early T2D toward kidney involvement, with higher glycemic burden, persistent obesity/insulin resistance, and albuminuria often in A2 while eGFR remains in G2. Blood pressure and triglycerides are typically higher, HDL lower, and medication patterns may include metformin plus increasing use of SGLT2 and/or insulin. Clinically, this profile is plausible as an intermediate-risk state where microvascular injury is measurable but advanced renal failure has not yet developed. It supports teaching on early diabetic nephropathy and multidomain risk coupling (glycemia, BP, lipids, kidney markers) [1-4,6-8].

**Long-duration type 2 diabetes with CKD 3b and macroalbuminuria (`advanced_t2d_ckd3b`)**: This phenotype captures advanced non-ESRD diabetic kidney disease: long diabetes duration, reduced eGFR in G3b range, marked albuminuria (often A3), and elevated creatinine/BUN. Glycemic control is often persistently above target despite treatment, with substantial cardiometabolic burden and more intensive medication patterns. The profile is realistic for complex outpatient diabetes-nephrology management and explicitly excludes ESRD by maintaining eGFR >=15. It is useful for discussing staging, therapeutic tradeoffs, and progression risk in advanced diabetic CKD [1-5,8].

**Type 1 diabetes with early nephropathy (`t1d_early_nephropathy`)**: This phenotype reflects long-standing T1D with low endogenous insulin production, insulin dependence, moderate hyperglycemia, and early renal injury (often A2 albuminuria with preserved or mildly reduced eGFR). Compared with severe T2D phenotypes, obesity and insulin-resistance lipid patterns may be less dominant, while the T1D signature is maintained through low C-peptide and insulin use requirements. It provides a clear teaching contrast between diabetes subtypes and shows how nephropathy can appear before major filtration decline [1-4,9].

**Type 1 diabetes, poor control, CKD 3a (`t1d_poor_control_ckd3a`)**: This phenotype represents high-risk, long-duration T1D with poor glycemic control, very low C-peptide, persistent insulin dependence, and clinically meaningful kidney impairment (typically G3a with A2/A3 albuminuria). Blood pressure is often elevated and renal chemistries reflect reduced kidney function while remaining non-ESRD by design. The phenotype is useful for teaching progressive microvascular disease in T1D and the heterogeneity of albuminuria even at similar eGFR levels [1-5,8,9].
